Click a header — it toggles its block and writes nothing. Click a screen row inside — it would go on air.

States, by hand

  • Rest, collapsed (entries 2, 6): right-pointing ink chevron at the trailing end, no circle, no face — the row keeps its own flat look.
  • Hover: the row's own --icon-hover-bg paints the whole header, chevron included (FR-011) — no separate line wrapper.
  • Expanded (entry 3): chevron rotated 90° down over 200 ms var(--ease); the block morphs open over 230 ms. Click the header again to close it.
  • Live (entry 1): no chevron — the open block and --sidebar-selected-bg tint carry the state; its header click does nothing (it can't be collapsed). Switch the live radio to watch the chevron appear and disappear across a crossing.
  • Section break (entry 4) and empty block (entry 5): no chevron, click is a no-op (FR-010).
  • Reduce: tick the checkbox — every flip and morph becomes an instant swap (FR-003/FR-005).
  • Jump: click a screen row inside an open block — the footer says what would go on air; that is the only projection path from the list (FR-004).

What never happens here

  • A header click never projects — no display write, no scroll, no selection change (FR-002/FR-007).
  • Nothing persists: open states are page memory; a reload paints the base rule (A2).
  • No plus or minus glyph, ever (FR-003).

The footer records every click the way the runner would log it — "toggle, no write" vs "would project screen N". A static page cannot put anything on air; it shows exactly which of the two a click is.
